# HomeDIYCalc — Market Research

**Project:** HomeDIYCalc — free online home improvement / DIY / construction material calculator website.
**Positioning:** "Calculate Materials. Estimate Costs. Build Your Shopping List."
**Target market:** United States (primary); UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand (secondary).
**Constraints:** No registration, no login, no backend, no paid API.
**Audience:** Homeowners, DIY enthusiasts, handymen, small contractors.
**Research date:** 20 November 2026
**Researcher note:** All figures in this document are labelled **Verified** (named public source + date), **Reported / second-hand** (a named source quoting another dataset, not independently checkable here), **Site-published** (a figure a competitor publishes about its own pages, methodology undisclosed), or **Unverified** (no public free source found — no number invented). Where two named sources disagree, both numbers are shown and the disagreement is flagged rather than averaged away.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Demand is real and concentrated.** One competitor's own study of 169 US home-improvement calculator terms totals **~2.1 million searches/month**, and **37% of it is concrete alone** (CalcWright, published 2026-06-05). The single phrase "concrete calculator" is ~550,000/month (US) in that study and corroborated by an independent free keyword tool.
- **Searchers want a tool, not an article.** 96% of the 169 terms literally contain the word "calculator"; only ~1% are phrased as questions and only 14% mention cost/price (CalcWright study). The winning page is a fast, working calculator first, with explanation underneath — not a blog post with a calculator attached.
- **The head terms are owned by giants; the entry point is the long tail.** "Concrete calculator" and "square footage calculator" are dominated by inchcalculator.com (est. ~2.8M visits/month, ~1.39M organic keywords per SEMrush via HypeStat) and calculator.net / CalculatorSoup. But the market is fragmenting fast: in 2026 alone a wave of small, focused, free, no-signup calculator sites (CalcWright, BuildMeter, MaterialCalc.io, PorchMath, CalcMyProject and many clones) is ranking for the same terms, and at least one niche SEO analysis reports small sites dominating the top 10 for "cubic yard calculator" (KD 18%).
- **HomeDIYCalc's defensible wedge is not "another concrete calculator".** It is (a) the *shopping-list* outcome in the positioning — quantity → purchasable units (bags, boxes, gallons, sheets) → printable list — which most competitors state but few make the hero, and (b) breadth across one coherent cluster (concrete + area/volume + landscape + paint/tile/drywall) with consistent UX, imperial + metric, and deep FAQ content per page. MaterialCalc.io already charges $2.99 for a PDF shopping list and $4.95/mo for Pro — HomeDIYCalc giving the shopping list away free is a direct differentiator.
- **Monetisation reality check:** every competitor monetises with display ads (AdSense where named); only MaterialCalc.io has a direct paid upsell. Reported CPCs in this niche are low ($0.10 for "cubic yard calculator" per a second-hand Semrush report; $1.42 for "rubber mulch calculator"; $1.78 for "calculate sq footage"). This is a **volume + ads** business, not a high-CPC one. Do not plan revenue around affiliate links to retailers until traffic exists — no competitor in the set shows a working retailer-affiliate model as its main income.

### 2.7 The wider field (observed, not deep-dived)

The 2026 wave of small focused calculator sites is itself a market signal: PorchMath (35 calculators), CalcMyProject (71), SiteCalc (84), BuildersMeasures (60+), CalcTide (52), MaterialMath (63), BuildNumerics (97), ProjectCalcs (76), CalcThis (13), HomePlanningHub, RenoCalcs, DIY Concrete Calculator and others all offer the same core set (concrete, paint, flooring, tile, gravel, mulch, drywall) with the same pitch — free, no signup, formulas shown. **Differentiation in this market is no longer "has a concrete calculator"; it is speed, correctness, breadth of long tail, and the outcome (shopping list / cost / shareable result).** Any new entrant should assume 5–10 competent lookalike sites will exist for every head term.

## 3. Keyword research — 55 candidates

### 3.1 How to read this table (please read before using the numbers)

- **Volume** = average monthly searches, **United States**, Google, unless stated otherwise.
- **Verified** = the number appears in a named public source accessed 2026-11-20 (source + date in the row). "Verified" here means *the source really publishes that number* — all keyword volumes are estimates made by the source's data provider, never exact Google counts. Two named sources disagree on "concrete slab calculator" (49,500 vs. 24,000) and on "concrete calculator" (550,000 vs. 410,000 average) — both are shown; the gap is a methodology/date difference, not an error to hide.
- **Reported / second-hand** = a named source quoting another dataset (e.g. Semrush via a Facebook post). Usable as a directional signal, not as a verified figure.
- **Site-published** = a competitor publishes the figure on its own pages with no disclosed methodology. Directional only.
- **Unverified** = no public free source found in this research (20 Nov 2026). No number has been invented for these rows. Keyword difficulty (KD) is **unverified for every row** except where a source is named — free public sources checked in this research (KWS Trends, CalcWright study) do not publish KD, and no paid tool (Ahrefs/Semrush) was available. Do not quote a KD for these rows without running them through a paid tool.
- **Seasonality** is only stated where a source gives monthly data; elsewhere it is marked as an assumption to test, not a fact.
- **Geography:** US is the primary market throughout. Where a source gives a country split, it is noted (US share of worldwide demand is 65–89% for the terms where it was published).

### 3.5 What could not be verified (honest gaps)

- **Keyword difficulty:** no free public source checked publishes KD for these terms. The only KD figures in this document are second-hand (cubic yard calculator KD 18%, Semrush via Facebook 2026-10-27) and as-presented-by-Clicks.so (calculate sq footage difficulty 45). **Before committing build order beyond the head terms, run the §3.4 list through a paid tool (Ahrefs/Semrush) or Google Keyword Planner with a free Ads account** — Keyword Planner gives volume ranges, not KD, but it is the closest thing to a primary source.
- **Google Trends absolute data:** Google Trends gives relative interest (0–100), not volumes, and its pages could not be scraped into citable numbers in this research. The only Trends-derived numbers used here are second-hand (Accio summary: "garage floor epoxy" peaked at 98 in Aug 2025; "concrete floor paint" rose 22→38 Nov 2024–Aug 2025 — reported, not verified). Seasonality claims in §3.3 that cite KWS Trends monthly data are the verified exception.
- **Competitor traffic:** only inchcalculator.com has public traffic estimates (Similarweb/HypeStat via search results). No traffic figures were found for calculator.net's construction pages (it has none), CalcWright, BuildMeter or MaterialCalc.io. Do not quote traffic for those four.
- **CPC:** only three figures exist in this research — $1.78 (calculate sq footage, Clicks.so), $1.42 (rubber mulch calculator, KWS Trends), $0.10 (cubic yard calculator, second-hand Semrush). All point the same way: **low CPCs**. No CPC was found for concrete/paint/tile/drywall head terms.

### 4.2 Long-tail opportunities (build second, in families)

- **Concrete long tail (site-published volumes, directional only):** bag calculator (6,600), block (5,400), yardage (5,400), footing (1,900), cylinder/post (1,300), sidewalk (850), steps (770), patio (750), wall (720). Each is a cheap page off the concrete hub engine; together they exceed 20,000/mo on site-published figures alone. Post-hole concrete is additionally flagged as "borderline winnable" in a Sep 2026 SERP probe.
- **Question/FAQ long tail:** "how many bags of mulch in a cubic yard", "how much paint for a 12×12 room" — ~1% of searches are questions, so these belong as **FAQ content on the calculator page**, not standalone pages. Exception: "how many bags of concrete do I need" is brand-walled (calculator.net / Omni / Inch in top 10, Sep 2026 probe) — do not target it as a page.
- **Accessory long tail (BuildMeter's playbook):** grout, mortar, thinset, tile spacers, backer-board screws, rebar, formwork, sealer. Individually tiny, collectively a moat — but only after the core families exist. BuildMeter's 444 tools show this is a volume game; HomeDIYCalc should not chase it at launch.
- **Cost variants:** only 14% of study terms mention cost/price. Build cost as an **output** on every quantity page (with user-editable unit prices, as MaterialCalc.io and BuildMeter do), not as separate "cost calculator" pages — except one: concrete driveway/slab cost, where Inch Calculator's cost-guide cluster proves demand exists.

### 4.3 Market risks

1. **Head-term brand wall.** "Concrete calculator" and "square footage calculator" top 10s are held by inchcalculator.com, calculator.net/CalculatorSoup and Omni Calculator — high-authority generalists. A new domain will not rank for these in months. Plan for 6–12 months of long-tail-first growth; measure success by long-tail rankings, not head-term positions.
2. **Commoditisation / clone wave.** At least a dozen near-identical free calculator sites launched or scaled in 2026 with the same pitch. Google can only rank so many templated "concrete calculator" pages. Differentiation must be real (shopping list, correct metric, speed, original data) — a 56th clone will not rank.
3. **AI Overviews and zero-click answers.** Simple "how much concrete" questions are exactly what AI Overviews answer inline. The defence is the **interactive tool itself** (which an overview cannot replace) and the shopping list / purchasable-unit output. Pure informational content ("how to calculate concrete") is the most exposed.
4. **Seasonality whiplash.** Verified swings are large: concrete 2.3× (Dec→Mar), roofing 3.4× (Oct→Apr), rubber mulch 7.5× (Dec→Apr). Winter traffic will fall sharply for outdoor materials. Mitigate with year-round terms (square footage, paint interior, drywall, tile) and by launching outdoor tools before spring, not during it.
5. **Low monetisation per visit.** Reported CPCs are $0.10–$1.78; display ads are the only proven model in the set, and HypeStat's ~$415/day for Inch Calculator is an estimate for a 2.8M-visit site — i.e. roughly $0.15/visit/day-rate territory at best, and a new site will earn far less per visit. MaterialCalc.io's $2.99 PDF / $4.95 Pro is the only direct-revenue proof point, and it is unproven at scale. **Do not forecast revenue from CPCs; forecast from traffic × a conservative RPM, and treat the PDF/Pro path as an experiment.**
6. **Data accuracy and trust.** Competitors already show visible errors (CalcWright's metric unit-mixing; MaterialCalc.io's 10×10 slab row showing 1.50 yd³ vs. the standard 1.23 yd³ before waste). One wrong bag count destroys trust in a calculator. Every formula needs a stated source (ACI/ASTM/manufacturer coverage), a worked example, and a visible waste factor — and metric/imperial must be tested, not templated.
7. **Secondary markets are smaller and different.** US is 65–89% of worldwide demand for the terms where a split was published (KWS Trends). UK/CA/AU/NZ matter, but they use metric, different bag sizes (25/40/50 kg vs. 40/60/80 lb) and different terminology (e.g. "plasterboard" vs. "drywall"). A US-first launch with a correct metric toggle covers most of the secondary demand; localised bag-size presets are a phase-2 item, not launch.
8. **No-backend constraint cuts both ways.** It is a strength (fast, private, no signup friction — exactly what the CalcWright study says users reward) and a limit: no saved projects, no accounts, no server-side price data. MaterialCalc.io's Pro (saved projects/bundles) exists precisely because of that gap. HomeDIYCalc can close it client-side (localStorage saved projects, shareable links with inputs in the URL like CalcWright's) without a backend.

### 4.4 Recommended launch order (summary)

1. **Wave 1 (before spring):** square footage, concrete (hub), cubic yard, gravel, mulch, paint.
2. **Wave 2:** concrete long tail (slab, pad, bag, footing, post hole, patio, driveway, steps), tile, drywall, flooring, brick/block, grout.
3. **Wave 3:** roofing/shingle, fencing, deck, retaining wall, sod/topsoil/sand, insulation, cost-guide companions for the top 3 hubs.
4. **Always:** one original data/methodology page (CalcWright-study model) for links; FAQ schema on every page (verify with a checker first); shareable result links; printable shopping list free on every page.
