> Status: Phase 5 — all 31 formulas (10 P0 validated by Vitest in Phase 2, 8 P1 validated by Vitest in Phase 3, 7 P2 validated by Vitest in Phase 4, all 2026-11-20, 1 Stair formula added in Phase 15, and 5 landscaping formulas added in Phase 17 — Sand, Raised Garden Bed Soil, Sod / Turf, Lawn Seed, and Fence, §§27–31) are implemented and **re-validated in Phase 5 (2026-11-20)**: every worked example was hand-computed and compared with the program's output, every formula test file contains a worked-example test, and the full Vitest suite passes 201/201. See the Phase 2, Phase 3, and Phase 4 reference tables below and each formula's validation status.
>
> Conventions used throughout:
> - Canonical internal units: feet (length), square feet (area), cubic feet (volume), US gallons (liquid volume). Metric inputs are converted to canonical units on entry via the unit engine.
> - **Package quantities always round up:** `package qty = ceil(required ÷ package capacity)`.
> - **Cost = rounded package qty × unit price** (cost is computed from what the user actually buys, not the raw required amount). If no price is entered, no cost is shown.
> - Waste is applied to the required amount before packaging: `adjusted = required × (1 + waste%)`.
> - Displayed quantities round to a sensible precision; full precision is kept internally until the final display step.
> - Constants: 1 yd³ = 27 ft³ (exact); 1 ft = 0.3048 m (exact); 1 US gal ≈ 3.785411784 L; 1 ft² ≈ 0.09290304 m².

## 1. Concrete Calculator — `/calculators/concrete-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `volume (ft³) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft)`; `volume (yd³) = volume (ft³) ÷ 27`.
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; outputs in yd³, ft³, and m³.
- **Waste:** user-editable percentage; applied to volume before packaging.
- **Packages:** ready-mix by the cubic yard, rounded up to the next 0.25 yd³ by default (the usual supplier increment — see the Phase 2 reference table above; whole-yard rounding is offered as an option) and/or bag counts (QUIKRETE yields in the Phase 2 reference table above).
- **Rounding:** volume displayed to 2 decimal places; yd³ rounded up per the packaging rule above.
- **Limitations:** Assumes a level, rectangular pour of uniform depth. Does not account for slope, uneven subgrade, spillage, or over-excavation beyond the waste allowance.
- **Worked example:** 10 ft × 10 ft slab, 4 in (⅓ ft) deep: 10 × 10 × ⅓ = 33.33 ft³ = 1.23 yd³. With 10% waste: 1.36 yd³ → order 1.5 yd³ (quarter-yard rounding).
- **Validation status:** validated by Vitest in Phase 2 (2026-11-20) — worked example, boundary cases, and rounding-up behavior all pass; re-validated in Phase 5 (2026-11-20): worked example hand-checked against the program's output, Vitest 201/201 pass; see `docs/QA-REPORT.md`.

## 2. Square Footage Calculator — `/calculators/square-footage-calculator/`

- **Formula:** rectangle `area (ft²) = length (ft) × width (ft)`; circle `area = π × r²`; triangle `area = ½ × base × height`; L-shape `area = L₁ × W₁ + L₂ × W₂` (the two rectangles added together).
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; outputs in ft², m², yd², and acres.
- **Waste:** not applicable (pure area measurement); the result feeds other calculators' waste inputs.
- **Rounding:** area displayed to 2 decimal places.
- **Limitations:** Measures flat area only; does not deduct openings (that is the Paint calculator's job) or account for irregular shapes beyond the supported shapes.
- **L-shape split:** the two rectangles must not overlap — any shared area would be counted twice. Split the room along the inside corner so the rectangles only meet at an edge.
- **Worked example:** 12 ft × 15 ft room: 12 × 15 = 180 ft² (≈ 16.72 m²). L-shape: 12 × 10 ft plus 6 × 8 ft = 120 + 48 = 168 ft².
- **Validation status:** validated by Vitest in Phase 2 (2026-11-20) — worked example, boundary cases, and rounding-up behavior all pass; re-validated in Phase 5 (2026-11-20): worked example hand-checked against the program's output, Vitest 201/201 pass; see `docs/QA-REPORT.md`.

## 3. Paint Calculator — `/calculators/paint-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `net area (ft²) = gross wall area − doors/windows area` (where entered); `paint volume = net area × coats ÷ coverage (ft²/gal)`. A standard door deducts 21 ft² — the true 3 × 7 ft area. (The Drywall Calculator, §11, deducts 20 ft² per door instead, as a drywall estimating convention.)
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; outputs in US gallons (and liters); coverage editable in ft²/gal or m²/L. Coverage is an area-per-volume rate, not a pure area: 350 ft²/gal = 8.59 m²/L (1 ft²/gal = 0.09290304 ÷ 3.785411784 m²/L ≈ 0.024542 m²/L), so the metric default is 8.59 m²/L, not the pure-area figure 32.52.
- **Reference coverage:** 350 ft²/gal per coat (8.59 m²/L in metric) — the low end of the 350–400 ft²/gal range on Sherwin-Williams interior latex data sheets (see the Phase 2 reference table above). The field is user-editable and the reference is labeled as typical for that manufacturer's paint, not guaranteed for any paint.
- **Waste:** user-editable percentage, applied to paint volume.
- **Packages:** gallons rounded up (whole gallons).
- **Cost:** `cost = rounded gallons × price per gallon` (price optional).
- **Rounding:** gallons rounded up to whole gallons for purchase; raw volume shown to 2 decimal places.
- **Limitations:** Coverage varies by surface, color change, and application method; the reference value is an estimate. Does not include primer (see Primer Calculator).
- **Worked example:** net area 400 ft², 2 coats, coverage 350 ft²/gal: 400 × 2 ÷ 350 = 2.29 gal → buy 3 gal. With 10% waste: 2.51 gal → still 3 gal.
- **Validation status:** validated by Vitest in Phase 2 (2026-11-20) — worked example, boundary cases, and rounding-up behavior all pass; re-validated in Phase 5 (2026-11-20): worked example hand-checked against the program's output, Vitest 201/201 pass; see `docs/QA-REPORT.md`.

## 4. Gravel Calculator — `/calculators/gravel-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `volume (ft³) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft)`; `volume (yd³) = volume (ft³) ÷ 27`; `weight = volume × density`.
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; outputs in yd³, ft³, m³, and tons (US short tons) / kg.
- **Reference density:** 2,800 lb/yd³ (≈ 1.4 US tons/yd³) for general gravel / #57 crushed stone, loose and dry — the usual figure in aggregate-supplier tables (see the Phase 2 reference table above; range roughly 2,400–3,000 lb/yd³). The field is user-editable.
- **Waste:** user-editable percentage (compaction/settling allowance), applied to volume.
- **Packages:** bulk by the cubic yard (rounded up); bags by weight where a bag size is entered.
- **Cost:** `cost = rounded yd³ × price per yd³` (price optional).
- **Rounding:** yd³ to 2 decimal places for display, rounded up for ordering; tons to 2 decimal places.
- **Limitations:** Density varies by stone type, size, and moisture; compaction and settling are only approximated by the waste allowance.
- **Worked example:** 10 ft × 10 ft area, 3 in (0.25 ft) deep: 10 × 10 × 0.25 = 25 ft³ = 0.93 yd³. At 2,800 lb/yd³: the unrounded 0.926 yd³ × 2,800 = 2,593 lb ≈ 1.30 US tons (before the 5% compaction allowance; 2,722 lb / 1.36 US short tons at the default).
- **Validation status:** validated by Vitest in Phase 2 (2026-11-20) — worked example, boundary cases, and rounding-up behavior all pass; re-validated in Phase 5 (2026-11-20): worked example hand-checked against the program's output, Vitest 201/201 pass; see `docs/QA-REPORT.md`.

## 5. Mulch Calculator — `/calculators/mulch-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `volume (ft³) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft)`; `volume (yd³) = volume (ft³) ÷ 27`; `bags = ceil(volume (ft³) ÷ bag volume (ft³))`.
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; outputs in yd³, ft³, m³, and bag count (standard bag = 2 ft³ — user-editable).
- **Reference density:** mulch is normally sold by volume, not weight; a weight output is optional and only shown if a density is entered. Typical bagged mulch density is not assumed — no invented density value is used.
- **Waste:** user-editable percentage, applied to volume.
- **Packages:** bulk yd³ rounded up; bags rounded up via `ceil`.
- **Cost:** `cost = rounded qty × unit price` (price per yd³ or per bag, optional).
- **Rounding:** yd³ to 2 decimal places for display; bags are whole numbers rounded up.
- **Limitations:** Does not account for settling/compaction beyond the waste allowance; depth should be measured after spreading, not in the pile.
- **Worked example:** 10 ft × 10 ft bed, 3 in (0.25 ft) deep: 25 ft³ = 0.93 yd³. With the default 5% settling allowance: 26.25 ft³, so in 2 ft³ bags: ceil(26.25 ÷ 2) = 14 bags. (With no settling allowance, ceil(25 ÷ 2) = 13 bags.)
- **Validation status:** validated by Vitest in Phase 2 (2026-11-20) — worked example, boundary cases, and rounding-up behavior all pass; re-validated in Phase 5 (2026-11-20): worked example hand-checked against the program's output, Vitest 201/201 pass; see `docs/QA-REPORT.md`.

## 10. Brick Calculator — `/calculators/brick-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `wall area (ft²) = length (ft) × height (ft)` (minus openings where entered); `bricks = ceil(wall area ÷ area per brick including mortar joint × (1 + waste%))`. Area per brick including joint = `(brick length + joint) × (brick height + joint)`. `mortar volume (ft³) = pre-waste brick count × joint volume per brick ÷ 1,728 × (1 + mortar waste%)` — the pre-waste count, because brick waste is breakage, not extra joints. `mortar bags = max(ceil(bricks to buy ÷ bricks per bag), ceil(installed bricks × (1 + mortar waste%) ÷ bricks per bag))`, where bricks per bag is the QUIKRETE maximum of up to 37 standard bricks per 80 lb bag (Mortar Mix No. 1102 data sheet) — the larger governs, so the recommendation never undercuts the manufacturer maximum. The data sheet states no cubic-foot yield per bag and no waste allowance: brick breakage is counted once, in the brick count only, and the mortar waste allowance derates the rating only. The same rating rule is used by the Mortar Calculator, so both return the same bag count for the same wall and waste. **The rating applies only to standard geometry — modular brick (7⅝ × 2¼ × 3⅝ in) at the standard ⅜ in joint or narrower, within a 1% tolerance that absorbs metric rounding (9.5 mm, 194 × 57 × 92 mm).** A wider joint needs more mortar per brick while fitting fewer bricks per ft², so the count-based figure falls exactly as the mortar volume rises (80 ft² wall, 15% mortar waste: ⅜ in joint → 5.09 ft³ and 18 bags; ¾ in joint → 8.82 ft³ but only 15 bags by the old rule) — the rating does not apply there, and neither does it for non-modular brick sizes. For such geometry the bag count comes from a verified user-entered yield (ft³ per bag, from the bag's own data sheet): `bags = ceil(waste-adjusted mortar volume ÷ yield)`. With no yield entered, no bag count, bag cost, or shopping-list bag line is shown. A narrower joint is safe: it uses no more mortar per brick than the standard case, so the rating stays a conservative basis (¼ in joint → 3.58 ft³, 19 bags).
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; brick size in inches or mm; outputs in brick count (and pallets where a pallet size is entered).
- **Reference brick size:** US standard modular brick 7⅝ in × 2¼ in × 3⅝ in with a ⅜ in mortar joint — Brick Industry Association Technical Note 10 (see the Phase 2 reference table above). The field is user-editable. Note: the face-area method used here gives 6.86 bricks/ft², while BIA's coursing table lists 6.75/ft² (675 per 100 ft²) for the same brick — a 1.6% difference, noted on the page.
- **Waste:** user-editable percentage (typical 5–10% for breakage/cuts — stated as guidance); applied before rounding.
- **Packages:** bricks rounded up; pallets via `ceil` where a pallet quantity is entered.
- **Cost:** `cost = (rounded bricks × price per brick) + (bags × price per bag)` (each price optional; an unpriced component adds nothing). Partial cost (Phase 8, 2026-11-22): when the bag count is unknown (rating does not apply, no verified yield entered) and only a mortar price was entered, the cost is suppressed — never $0. When a brick price was entered, the bricks-only figure is shown labeled "Partial cost (bricks only)" and explicitly excludes mortar, in the results row and in the copied shopping list. The partial flag is preserved through My Project (Phase 8 review response, ADR-16): the saved item keeps `costPartial`/`costLabel`/`costDetail`, the item line, room subtotal, and project total all label it partial, the text export and the CSV "Cost note" column carry the exclusion, and editing the unit price clears the flag (the edited price is the user's own figure).
- **Rounding:** brick count is a whole number rounded up.
- **Limitations:** Single-wythe wall assumption unless a wall-thickness input is added in Phase 2; does not give a site-mix breakdown (cement, lime, sand — see Mortar Calculator) or structural requirements.
- **Worked example:** 10 ft × 8 ft wall = 80 ft². Modular brick with ⅜ in joint: face area per brick = (7.625 + 0.375) × (2.25 + 0.375) in = 8 × 2.625 = 21 in² = 0.1458 ft². Bricks = 80 ÷ 0.1458 ≈ 549. With 5% waste: 576 bricks. Mortar: 549 bricks × 13.93 in³ ÷ 1,728 × 1.15 = 5.09 ft³. Bags: max(576 ÷ 37 = 16, 549 × 1.15 ÷ 37 = 18) = 18 bags. At the rating's implied yield (37 × 0.00806 ≈ 0.30 ft³ per bag), 18 bags provide ≈ 5.37 ft³, covering the 5.09 ft³. *(Verified by unit tests in Phase 2; bag method corrected in the PR #2 review response, 2026-11-22 — the old 9-bag volume figure is removed.)* With a ¾ in joint the same wall needs 8.82 ft³ of mortar and the rating no longer applies — no bag count is shown unless a verified yield is entered (8.82 ÷ 0.3 = 30 bags at a 0.3 ft³-per-bag yield). With a ¼ in joint it needs 3.58 ft³ and 19 bags from the rating.
- **Validation status:** validated by Vitest in Phase 2 (2026-11-20) — worked example, boundary cases, and rounding-up behavior all pass; re-validated in Phase 5 (2026-11-20): worked example hand-checked against the program's output, Vitest 201/201 pass; see `docs/QA-REPORT.md`.

## 26. Stair Calculator — `/calculators/stair-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `risers = ceil(total rise ÷ maximum riser height)`; `riser height = total rise ÷ risers` (every riser the same height); `treads = risers − 1` (the upper floor is the last tread); `total run = treads × tread depth`; `stringer length = √(total rise² + total run²)`; `stair angle = atan(total rise ÷ total run)`.
- **Units:** Inches for every length; angle in degrees. Metric inputs are converted to inches on entry via the unit engine and converted back for display.
- **Reference values:** 2021 IRC limits — maximum riser height 7.75 in (R311.7.5.1), minimum tread depth 10 in (R311.7.5.2), maximum single-flight rise 12 ft 7 in (151 in, R311.7.3), minimum headroom 6 ft 8 in (R311.7.2), minimum landing depth 36 in in the direction of travel (R311.7.6). The maximum riser height is editable only downward: a value above 7.75 in is rejected. The 2024 IRC renumbers the stair section to R318.7 with the dimensions unchanged (as reported by secondary sources; the ICC text was not read for this page).
- **Waste:** not applicable in the usual sense — the riser count rounds up by construction so no riser exceeds the maximum, and there is no waste-allowance input.
- **Packages:** not applicable — the calculator returns riser/tread counts, total run, stringer length, and stair angle; no material quantities and no bag/box counts.
- **Cost:** not applicable — no prices are entered on this calculator and no cost is shown.
- **Rounding:** riser count is a whole number rounded up; riser height, total run, and stringer length are shown to 2 decimal places; stair angle is shown to 1 decimal place.
- **Limitations:** Straight stairs only — no winders, landings, or turns. A preliminary planning estimate; it does not guarantee compliance with local building code, which may adopt a different code edition or amend the IRC limits, and it does not certify a stair as code-compliant or produce cut-ready stringer designs (cut angles and connection details are not calculated; stringer sizing, lumber species and grade, and support details need a qualified professional or the local code's span tables). It does not check headroom, stair width, handrails, or guards. A rise over 151 in needs an intermediate landing: the calculator warns but still returns the straight-flight numbers as a planning estimate.
- **Worked example:** total rise 105 in (8 ft 9 in), 10.5 in tread: 105 ÷ 7.75 = 13.55, so 14 risers at 105 ÷ 14 = 7.5 in each; 13 treads × 10.5 in = 136.5 in of run (11 ft 4.5 in); stringer √(105² + 136.5²) = √29,657.25 = 172.21 in (about 14 ft 4 in); angle atan(105 ÷ 136.5) = 37.57° (about 37.6°).
- **Validation status:** validated by Vitest in Phase 15 — 24 tests in `src/lib/formulas/stair.test.ts` covering the worked example, the IRC defaults, exactly-divisible and stricter-maximum-riser cases, rejection of a tread under 10 in and a maximum riser over 7.75 in, and the 151 in single-flight limit boundary (including the exact metric boundary 383.54 cm); see `docs/QA-REPORT.md`.

## 27. Sand Calculator — `/calculators/sand-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `volume (ft³) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft)`; `adjusted (ft³) = volume (ft³) × (1 + waste%)`; `adjusted (yd³) = adjusted (ft³) ÷ 27`; `bulk to order (yd³) = ceil(adjusted (yd³))`; `weight needed (lb) = adjusted (yd³) × density (lb/yd³)` — the weight of the sand required, from the unrounded volume; `weight needed (tons) = weight needed (lb) ÷ 2,000`; `order weight (lb) = bulk to order (yd³) × density (lb/yd³)` — the weight of the rounded bulk purchase quantity, always ≥ the weight needed; `order weight (tons) = order weight (lb) ÷ 2,000`; `bagged weight (lb) = bags × bag size (ft³) ÷ 27 × density (lb/yd³)` — the estimated weight of the bagged purchase quantity, a density-based estimate (the bag's stated weight governs; a 50 lb bag is about 0.5 ft³); `bagged weight (tons) = bagged weight (lb) ÷ 2,000`; `bags = ceil(adjusted (ft³) ÷ bag size (ft³))`. Moisture moves the figure more than the sand type does: Engineering ToolBox, "Ditch Filling Materials — Densities" — https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/amp/ditch-filling-materials-d_1189.html — gives dry sand 100, damp 115, wet 120 lb/ft³ — damp sand runs about 10–15% heavier than dry sand, wet sand up to about 20% heavier. No per-type density is assumed: sand types differ mainly in grain size and use, not in a fixed weight. A 50 lb bag is about 0.5 ft³, and 54 × 50 lb bags make one yd³. The density field defaults to 2,700 lb/yd³ (dry sand) and is editable; the load-ticket figure from the supplier governs. Playground surfacing depth: US CPSC Handbook for Public Playground Safety, Pub. No. 325, §2.4.2.2 Table 2 — https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325.pdf — 9 in of compressed sand protects to a 4 ft fall height, and never less than 9 in of loose-fill material (except 6 in for shredded/recycled rubber); those depths are guidelines for public playgrounds, not a mandatory rule, and they are compressed depths that must be maintained — fresh fill settles, so install deeper and top up. Paver bedding sand is exactly 1 in (ICPI — already on the Paver page).

- **Packages:** bulk yd³ rounded up to whole yards for ordering; bags rounded up via `ceil`.
- **Cost:** cost follows the "Buy as" choice: bulk is priced at the bulk price per cubic yard, bags at the bag price — a buyer buys bulk or bags, never both. No cost is shown when the price for the chosen form is not entered; the other form's cost is shown only as a labelled comparison (prices optional).
- **Rounding:** yd³ to 2 decimal places for display; bags are whole numbers rounded up.
- **Limitations:** Both the volume and the weight are estimates: loose sand settles and compacts after spreading, moisture changes its volume and its weight, and sand varies by source and gradation. Damp sand runs about 10–15% heavier than dry sand, wet sand up to about 20% heavier — the weight shown is for the density entered, so enter the supplier's load-ticket density when the sand is wet. The volume is an estimate, not a surveyed quantity: uneven ground and compaction after spreading change the real depth. Weights here are estimates for ordering only — they do not show whether a vehicle can carry the load; check the vehicle's rated payload (on the driver's door placard) and local transport rules before hauling sand yourself. Does not cover compaction beyond the waste allowance. NOT for structural fill or concrete mix design — no engineering recommendations. For a patio job, the Paver Calculator already computes the 1 in bedding sand.

## 28. Raised Garden Bed Soil Calculator — `/calculators/raised-bed-soil-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `soil (ft³) = length (ft) × width (ft) × max(fill depth − filler depth, 0) (ft) × beds`; `ordered (ft³) = soil (ft³) × (1 + settling%)`; `ordered (yd³) = ordered (ft³) ÷ 27`; `bulk to order (yd³) = ceil(ordered (yd³))`; `bags = ceil(ordered (ft³) ÷ bag size (ft³))`; mix split on the ORDERED volume, normalized: `topsoil (ft³) = ordered (ft³) × topsoil% ÷ (topsoil% + compost% + aeration%)` (same for compost and aeration). - **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; outputs in ft³, yd³, m³, bag count, and mix parts in ft³.
- **Reference values:** 60/30/10 mix (topsoil/compost/aeration — the calculator's third part is perlite or coarse sand; Illinois's 10% is a soilless mix of peat moss, perlite, and/or vermiculite) — one example from Univ. of Illinois Extension's Illinois Vegetable Garden Guide raised-bed checklist (60% topsoil, 30% compost, 10% soilless growing mix), as reproduced in Illinois Times, 17 Apr 2014 — https://npaper-wehaa.com/illinoistimes/2014/04/17/?page=12&article=2204903, not a universal prescription; other extension services publish different starting points for the same job — Univ. of Maryland Extension, "Filling Your Raised Bed" — https://marylandgrows.umd.edu/2019/04/19/filling-your-raised-bed/ — gives compost and purchased topsoil at 1:2 or 1:1 for a bed on lawn, and compost and soilless growing mix at 1:1 for a bed on hardscape or heavily compacted soil (topsoil up to 20% by volume only in beds at least 16 in deep); Iowa State University Extension — https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/what-would-be-good-soil-mix-raised-bed — gives equal parts topsoil, organic matter, and coarse sand, or about 70% topsoil and 30% compost. The calculator normalizes the three figures so they always add to 100%, and the proportions are user-editable. Settling: UF/IFAS Extension, "Gardening in Raised Beds" (ENH1211) — https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP472 — advises filling to the very top because the level may drop by about 2 in once it settles, and topping the bed off after each planting season as the soil keeps shrinking; the calculator's settling allowance (default 10%) is an editable assumption, not a measured figure. Depth: Univ. of Maryland Extension gives at least 8 in of soil for leafy greens, beans, and cucumber, and 12–24 in for peppers, tomatoes, and squash, for beds on hardscape or compacted ground where the bed is the whole root zone; UF/IFAS says beds should be at least 6 in high. Fill depth is the soil depth, not the bed height. Bag sizes 1.5/2 ft³ are common at garden centers — read the bag.
- **Waste:** the settling allowance (default 10%) plays the waste role here — it is applied to the soil volume.
- **Packages:** bulk yd³ rounded up to whole yards for ordering; bags rounded up via `ceil`.
- **Cost:** cost follows the "Buy as" choice: premixed soil in bulk is priced at the bulk price per cubic yard, premixed soil in bags at the bag price — a buyer buys one form, not all three. No cost is shown when the price for the chosen form is not entered; the other form's cost is shown only as a labelled comparison. Buying the mix components separately has no cost here, because the parts carry no prices. The mix is composition for premixed soil — what is in the bag or the bulk load — and the purchase only for the components choice (prices optional).
- **Rounding:** ft³ to 2 decimal places for display; mix parts to 2 decimal places; bags are whole numbers rounded up.
- **Limitations:** The mix is a starting point, not a prescription — soil, compost, and perlite vary by supplier, and extension services disagree on the proportions (see the reference values above); some growers use no perlite at all. Settling is an assumption, not a measurement: a freshly filled bed settles (UF/IFAS: about 2 in) and keeps shrinking as the organic matter breaks down — top the beds up after each planting season rather than over-ordering in spring. Does not account for soil removed by digging or for a drainage layer. Pallet weight: Purdue — sod weighs about 35–40 lb per sq yd, so a 50 sq yd pallet is 1,750–2,000 lb; Mississippi State — a 450 ft² pallet may weigh as much as a ton. UK roll ≈ 1 m² — Lindum Turf specification sheet (Turfgrass Growers Association Standard, BS 3969) — https://docs.planning.org.uk/20230403/131/RRM9NZSJKMV00/04qn372o6tqbcemf.pdf — small rolls 1 m² (610 × 1,640 mm); Sherborne Turf — https://www.sherborneturf.co.uk/goods-calculator — each roll covers one square metre (about 170 × 59 cm). Roll and pallet coverage are user-editable — piece sizes vary by supplier and region.

- **Packages:** rolls and pallets rounded up via `ceil`.
- **Cost:** cost follows the "Buy as" choice: pallets are priced at the pallet price, rolls at the roll price — a buyer buys rolls or pallets, never both. No cost is shown when the price for the chosen form is not entered; the other form's cost is shown only as a labelled comparison (prices optional).

- **Limitations:** Piece sizes vary by supplier and region (US rolls 2 × 5 ft, Southern slabs 16 × 24 in, UK rolls ≈ 1 m²) — the coverage fields are editable for exactly this reason. ## 30. Lawn Seed Calculator — `/calculators/lawn-seed-calculator/`

- **Formula:** `net area (ft²) = max(length × width − exclusions, 0)`; `seed (lb) = net area ÷ 1,000 × rate (lb per 1,000 ft²)`; `seed with buffer (lb) = seed (lb) × (1 + buffer%)`; `seed (kg) = seed (lb) × 0.45359237`. The rate is entered FOR THE JOB PICKED — the bag-label new-lawn rate for a new lawn, the bag-label overseeding rate when overseeding — and is used exactly as entered: there is no job factor and the calculator applies no further reduction, because a manufacturer overseeding rate is already an overseeding rate and halving it again would under-seed the lawn. The job-type choice on the page is a label only; it changes the guidance text, never the arithmetic. The mixed-bag breakdown over the standard US bag sizes 25 / 10 / 3 lb is the least-overbuy mix (see Packages). If `net area ≤ 0`, the calculator returns zeros with a warning.
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/ft² or m/m²; the rate is enterable in lb per 1,000 ft² OR in g/m² and is converted in compute() via the unit engine — 1 lb per 1,000 ft² = 4.882 g/m² (453.59 g ÷ 92.90 m²); outputs in lb and kg, with g/m² equivalents shown in hints.
- **Reference values:** Penn State Extension, "Lawn Establishment", Table 1 — https://extension.psu.edu/lawn-establishment — new lawn: turf-type tall fescue 6–8, Kentucky bluegrass 2–3, perennial ryegrass 4–5, fine fescues 4–5 lb per 1,000 ft²; overseeding/renovation: perennial ryegrass 4–6 lb per 1,000 ft² (not half the new-lawn rate). NC State Extension, "Extension Gardener Handbook", ch. 9, Table 9–4 — https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/extension-gardener-handbook/9-lawns — tall fescue 6, Kentucky bluegrass 1.5–2, bermudagrass 1–2, zoysiagrass 1–2, centipedegrass 0.25–0.5, bahiagrass 5 lb per 1,000 ft². K-State Research and Extension MF736, "Tall Fescue Lawns" — https://bookstore.ksre.ksu.edu/download/tall-fescue-lawns_MF736 — tall fescue 6–8 lb per 1,000 ft² for a new lawn; overseeding 6–8 lb to repair large dead areas, half-rate 3–5 lb to thicken a thin lawn; September is the best time to plant tall fescue. Scotts Turf Builder Grass Seed Shady Areas Mix label — https://scottsmiraclegro.com/en-ca/brands/scotts/products/grass-seed/20240.html — 3–4 lb per 1,000 ft². Combined ranges used on the page: Kentucky bluegrass 1.5–3 (Penn State 2–3; NC State 1.5–2), tall fescue 6–8, perennial ryegrass 4–5, fine fescues 4–5, bermudagrass 1–2 lb per 1,000 ft². In g/m² (× 4.882): tall fescue 29–39, Kentucky bluegrass 7–15, perennial ryegrass 20–24, fine fescues 20–24, bermudagrass 5–10 for a new lawn; overseeding tall fescue 15–24 and perennial ryegrass 20–29 g/m² — the overseeding figure varies by grass, so no single overseeding g/m² figure is given. Overseeding is NOT simply half the new-lawn rate: Penn State lists perennial ryegrass at 4–6 lb for renovation, about the same as its 4–5 lb new-lawn rate, and K-State lists the full 6–8 lb for large dead areas — only thickening a thin tall-fescue lawn takes the half-rate 3–5 lb. Rates differ between universities for the same grass (NC State 6 lb vs Penn State 6–8 lb for tall fescue; Kentucky bluegrass 1.5–2 vs 2–3) — the ranges are guidance, and the bag label governs; never a single invented rate. Best time: cool-season grasses in late summer to early fall (mid-August to mid-October) beats spring (Univ. of Maryland Extension — https://extension.umd.edu/resource/seeding-or-sodding-lawns ; K-State calls September the best time for tall fescue); warm-season grasses in late spring to early summer.
- **Waste:** the buffer (default 10%) plays the waste role here — it is applied to the seed weight.
- **Packages:** seed bags are whole numbers from the mixed-bag breakdown over the standard US bag sizes 25 / 10 / 3 lb; the purchase weight is the breakdown total (what you buy), which is always ≥ the buffered need. The breakdown is the least-overbuy mix: for buffered amounts up to 10,000 lb every combination is searched in integer hundredths of a pound and the winner minimizes (1) total purchased weight, then (2) number of bags, then (3) prefers larger bags; above 10,000 lb the mix falls back to greedy largest-first, which still covers the need but is not guaranteed to be the least overbuy (a 1e-6 tolerance keeps floating-point noise from adding a hundredth of a pound). E.g. 38.5 lb → 3 × 10 lb + 3 × 3 lb (39 lb total, 6 bags); 19.25 lb → 2 × 10 lb (20 lb).
- **Cost:** cost follows the "Buy as" choice: bags are priced on the purchased bag weight (what you buy), bulk by the pound on the buffered weight (what you buy, with no bag rounding) — a buyer buys bags or bulk, never both (price optional).
- **Rounding:** seed weight shown to 1 decimal place (lb/kg) for display; bag counts are whole numbers.
- **Limitations:** Rates differ by species AND by university (Penn State vs NC State give different figures for the same grass — Kentucky bluegrass 2–3 vs 1.5–2 lb per 1,000 ft²) — the ranges here are guidance, and the bag label governs. Overseeding is not simply half the new-lawn rate: Penn State lists perennial ryegrass at 4–6 lb per 1,000 ft² for renovation, about the same as its new-lawn rate, and K-State lists the full 6–8 lb for large dead areas — only thickening a thin tall-fescue lawn takes the half-rate 3–5 lb. Seed weight also varies by species and coating. Does not account for spreader calibration, overlap, or watering — a poorly calibrated spreader can use far more seed than the calculation, and seed that dries out does not germinate. For an instant lawn, compare the Sod Calculator: sod costs more per square foot but typically establishes in 3–4 weeks after laying (roots in 2–3 weeks), while seed takes around 4–6 weeks to establish and longer to fill in fully (Univ. of Maryland Extension). Seedbed preparation is a separate job — the seed goes on prepared soil (see the Topsoil Calculator).
- **Worked example:** a 5,000 ft² new lawn at 7 lb per 1,000 ft² (tall fescue): 5 × 7 = 35 lb. With a 10% buffer that is 38.5 lb — buy it as 3 × 10 lb + 3 × 3 lb bags (6 bags, 39 lb total), the least overbuy from standard 25/10/3 lb bags.

## 31. Fence Calculator — `/calculators/fence-calculator/`

- **Formula (picket mode):** `net run (ft) = max(run length − gates and openings, 0)`; a gate or opening in the middle of the run splits the fence into separate runs — `segments` is the number of runs the net length is divided into (1 = one continuous run: no gate, or a gate at the end), the runs are assumed equal in length, and `segment length (ft) = net run ÷ segments`; `sections per run = ceil(segment length ÷ post spacing)`; `sections = sections per run × segments`; `posts = (sections per run + 1) × segments + end-gate support posts` — every run needs a post at both ends, and no post is shared across a gate opening; `gate posts = 2 × (segments − 1) + end-gate support posts` (one at the end of each run flanking each gate opening, plus the end-gate support post when counted); `line posts = posts − gate posts`; a freestanding end gate (`segments = 1` with a gate at the end) adds `end-gate support posts = 1` — the fence-end post alone cannot hold a gate — and 0 when the gate hangs from an existing support (house, wall, or existing post); with `segments > 1` the end-gate option is ignored because each gate opening already counts two flanking posts; `rails per section = 2 up to 4 ft high, 3 up to 6 ft, 4 above 6 ft` (with a 0.02 ft tolerance at the band boundaries, because the metric default of 1.83 m converts to 6.0039 ft and a 6 ft fence must still take 3 rails); `rails = ceil(sections × rails per section × (1 + waste%))`; `pickets = ceil((net run ÷ ((picket width + picket gap) ÷ 12)) × (1 + waste%))`; `concrete bags = posts × bags per post`. **Formula (panel mode):** `panels per run = ceil(segment length ÷ panel length)`; `panels = panels per run × segments`; `posts = (panels per run + 1) × segments + end-gate support posts`; `gate posts = 2 × (segments − 1) + end-gate support posts`; `line posts = posts − gate posts` (end-gate support posts follow the same rule as in picket mode); `gravel boards = panels × boards flag (1 with, 0 without)`; `Postcrete bags = posts × bags per post`; `cut length (ft) = segment length − (panels per run − 1) × panel length`; the last panel of a run is flagged cut when `cut length < panel length − 0.01 ft` (so an exact fit is not flagged), and `partial panels = segments` when any run needs a partial panel, else 0. If `net run ≤ 0`, the calculator returns zeros with a warning. Waste applies to pickets and rails only, never to posts.
- **Units:** Inputs in ft/in or m/cm; outputs in counts (posts, rails, pickets, panels, boards, bags) plus the segment fields (segments, segment length, sections/panels per run, gate posts, line posts, end-gate support posts, partial panels).
- **Reference values:** rails by height — 2 up to 4 ft, 3 up to 6 ft, 4 above 6 ft — is common practice for wood picket fences, not a manufacturer rule; post spacing 6–8 ft is typical for a wood picket fence. Concrete: Quikrete, "Setting Posts in Concrete" — https://www.quikrete.com/athome/settingposts.asp — sizes the hole about 3 times the post width and 1/3 to 1/2 of the above-ground post length deep, plus 6 in of gravel; its Fast-Setting Concrete Mix data sheet (Product No. 1004-50, rev. Nov 2025 — https://www.quikrete.com/pdfs/data_sheet-fast%20setting%20concrete%20mix%201004-50.pdf) works 2 × 50 lb bags for a 3 in post in a 9 in hole 30 in deep (50 lb bag yields ≈ 0.375 ft³); Sakrete's Fast Setting Concrete Mix data sheet — https://www.sakretetime.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/6455a920cb110da995a2df2d_Fast-Setting-Concrete.pdf — tables, for a 10 in hole (50 lb bags), 2 bags at 18 in deep, 2.5 at 24 in, and 3 at 30 in. An 80 lb bag of standard mix yields about 0.60 ft³ (QUIKRETE Concrete Mix No. 1101 data sheet — see the Phase 2 reference table above), so the default of 2 × 80 lb bags per post is a starting figure suited to a 4×4 post in a 10 in hole about 24 in deep only — deeper or wider holes need more, and the yield printed on the bag governs. Postcrete: Blue Circle Postcrete data sheet — https://assets.materialsmarket.com/technical-downloads/Blue+Circle+Postcrete+Cement+-+Datasheet.pdf — hole 3 times the post width, depth 1/3 of the fence height above ground; 2 × 20 kg bags for a 100 × 100 mm post in a 300 × 600 mm hole, so the default of 2 bags per post is a starting figure for that hole size only. Panel length: most UK panels are 6 ft / 1.83 m (Jacksons Fencing, "How To Reduce The Size (Width) of Fence Panel" — https://www.jacksons-fencing.co.uk/the-edit/how-you-can-reduce-the-size-width-of-a-fence-panel). Cutting: Grange Fencing's guarantee conditions — https://www.grangefencing.co.uk/about/ — say cutting treated wood renders its guarantee null and void unless a cut-end treatment is applied, and Suregreen's FAQ — https://www.sure-green.com/technical-area/technical-timber-products/technical-rounded-wooden-posts/frequently-asked-questions.html — notes cutting exposes untreated areas requiring treatment; Jacksons Fencing publishes panel-specific cut-down instructions and kits for the panels that can be shortened — check the panel maker's instructions before cutting, and do not assume a panel can be cut.
- **Waste:** user-editable allowance (default 10%), applied to pickets and rails only.
- **Packages:** counts are whole numbers rounded up via `ceil`; concrete and Postcrete bags are whole numbers (posts × bags per post).
- **Cost:** the sum of the priced parts only — `posts × price per post + pickets × price per picket + rails × price per rail` (picket mode) or `panels × price per panel + posts × price per post + Postcrete bags × price per bag` (panel mode); unpriced parts contribute 0, and no cost is shown when no price is entered. When a cost is shown but any priced part has no price entered, it is labelled a priced-materials subtotal, not a complete project cost. The cost always excludes gate hardware (hinges, latch), fasteners, labor, and delivery — none of those are counted anywhere on the page.
- **Rounding:** counts are whole numbers rounded up; the cut length is shown to 1 decimal place.
- **Limitations:** This calculator counts materials only. It does not check structural adequacy, wind load, or foundation design; it does not tell you how deep to set posts, what footing a post needs, or whether a fence complies with local rules; and it does not settle boundary placement — confirm the line and any permit with your local authority, and call 811 (US) before you dig. Post depth is deliberately not an output: the "one-third of the height" rule of thumb does not hold for all soils and wind loads, and a wrong depth is a structural failure, not a rounding error. The runs are assumed equal in length — the calculator cannot know the real segment lengths from the total gate width alone; if the gate is not centered, calculate each actual segment separately instead of relying on an equal-length estimate, and add the runs up. No post is shared across a gate opening: each run has its own end posts. A freestanding gate at the end of the run needs one extra support post — the fence-end post alone cannot hold a gate — unless the gate hangs from an existing support (house, wall, or existing post); the extra post counts as a gate post. Gate posts are counted as ordinary fence posts, so a heavy gate may need a larger post or a deeper footing — check the gate maker's instructions. Gate hinges and a latch are not counted anywhere on this page or in the cost. Concrete and Postcrete figures are starting figures, not sufficient quantities: the count depends on hole width and depth, post size, soil, wind exposure, frost depth, local requirements, and the yield printed on the bag. The rails-per-section rule is common practice, not a manufacturer rule. A run that is not a multiple of the panel length needs the last panel cut, and the cut piece is waste — do not assume the panel can be cut (see the reference values above); the calculator flags partial panels but does not add a spare panel. Any cost shown covers the priced materials only — gate hardware, fasteners, labor, delivery, and tools are never included, and any material without a price entered is left out of the total.
- **Worked example:** a 100 ft privacy fence, 6 ft high, 8 ft post spacing, 6 in pickets with no gap: 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5, so 13 sections and 14 posts; 13 sections × 3 rails = 39, +10% waste = 43 rails; 100 ft ÷ 0.5 ft per picket = 200, +10% waste = 220 pickets; 14 posts × 2 bags = 28 × 80 lb concrete bags. Panel mode: a 100 ft run with 6 ft panels is 17 panels, 18 posts, 17 gravel boards, and 36 Postcrete bags, with the last panel cut to 4 ft. With a 4 ft gate in the middle of a 20 ft run instead: 16 ft of fence in two 8 ft runs, 8 ft spacing gives 1 section per run and 4 posts — 2 line posts and 2 gate posts — where one continuous 16 ft run would count only 3 posts; 2 sections × 3 rails = 6, +10% waste = 7 rails; 16 ft ÷ 0.5 ft per picket = 32, +10% waste = 36 pickets; 4 posts × 2 bags = 8 concrete bags. ## Shared rules (all calculators)

- **Package qty = ceil(required ÷ package capacity).** Never round down a purchase quantity.
- **Cost = rounded qty × unit price.** Cost is computed from what the user buys; no price entered means no cost shown.
- **1 yd³ = 27 ft³; 1 US gal ≈ 3.785411784 L; board feet = T(in) × W(in) × L(ft) ÷ 12** (board foot formula documented here for reference; the Board Foot Calculator itself is P1).
- **Validation:** zero/negative/empty required inputs produce an inline error and no result.
